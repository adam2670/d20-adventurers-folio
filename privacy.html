Privacy Policy

D20 Adventurer’s Folio

Home Support Privacy

Last updated: 13 September 2026

The short version. D20 Adventurer’s Folio collects nothing about you. It has no accounts, no servers and no analytics, and nothing you make in it is sent to us — there is no “us” to send it to. One thing reaches beyond your device: if you choose to join a table, your device talks directly to the other devices in the room. What that sends, and to whom, is set out below.

What the app stores

Everything you create — characters, ability scores, inventories, notes and any portrait you choose — is written to the app’s own storage on your device. A small number of preferences (your chosen dice set, whether sound is on) and your recent dice rolls (the last 50, including the name of any roll the DM, the person running the game, asked you for) are stored the same way. Clear in the dice tray removes the roll history.

This data is included in your normal iPhone or iPad backup, which is governed by Apple’s privacy policy, not this one. It is not sent to us, because there is no “us” to send it to. Apart from a table you choose to join, and purchases, which belong to Apple, the app makes no network connections at all. Both are described below.

If you join a table, your device also remembers a random identifier of its own so the same table can recognize you next week. It is not derived from you or your device and it means nothing anywhere else.

Photographs

If you give a character a portrait, you pick it through Apple’s own photo picker. The app is never granted access to your photo library — Apple hands it only the single image you chose. That image is stored with the character on your device, and the full-size photograph never leaves it. The stored image goes with the character if you share or export it yourself. If you join a table, a small copy — about 256 pixels across — is sent to the DM so your character can be recognized, and passed to the other players in the room.

Playing at a table

This version can join a game run by a DM using D20 Chair. It is never automatic. The app makes no connection of any kind until you tap Join. Played on your own, the app makes no connection to anyone except Apple: it asks Apple at each launch which purchases you own (see Purchases below), and Apple may occasionally show its own prompt to rate the app.

When you join, your device talks directly to the other devices in the room, over your local Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth. There is no server of ours in between, we operate no service that sees your game, and nothing about your play reaches us. It does not work over the internet — everyone has to be in the same room.

What the DM’s device receives from yours. Your character sheet — all of it, including your current hit points, any conditions you have written down, your spell slots, and anything you have typed into it yourself, such as a background, adventure notes or what your character is carrying. A small copy of your character’s picture, if you set one. Anything you type to the DM privately. Your rolls, when you are asked for one.

What the other players see. Your character’s name, class, level and picture, whether you are at the table, and whether your character is healthy, bloodied or down. They do not see your exact hit points, your conditions, your spell slots, your sheet, or anything you and the DM say to each other. Those are addressed to the DM alone. If the DM is using a map, everyone at the table also sees where your token stands on it and whose turn it is.

What stays afterwards, and where. Your sheet is never stored by anyone else — it is sent fresh each time you join, and while you are playing your hit points, conditions and spell slots keep the DM’s screen up to date as they change. What the DM’s device does keep, so the table is still there next week, is your character’s name, class and level, and a picture for it. The DM can clear that at any time, and it is removed from their device rather than hidden. If they show the table a map, a copy of that picture is written to a temporary file on your device, and it is deleted when the table ends, or the next time you open the app if it was closed first.

Everything else on this page is unchanged. No accounts, no sign‑in, no analytics, no advertising, and nothing sold or shared with any company.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in or registration.
  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting SDKs.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking, and no data sold or shared with any company. The people at a table you join are not a third party in that sense — they are the people you chose to play with, and they see only what is listed above.
  • No location access.
  • No network connections except a table you join yourself, and Apple’s App Store for purchases.

Purchases

In-app purchases are handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. Apple tells the app only whether a purchase is owned; it never gives the app your name, your email or your payment details, and the app never sees or stores them. Purchase handling is covered by Apple’s privacy policy.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Your rights

Because no personal data is collected or held, there is nothing for us to disclose, correct or delete on request. You can remove everything the app has stored at any time by deleting the app from your device — though copies inside your own iPhone, iPad or computer backups stay until those backups age out or you delete them.

Changes

If a future version ever collects anything, or transmits anything anywhere, this policy will be updated before that version is submitted for review, and the change will be described here rather than buried.

This website

The policy above is about the app. This site is three static pages hosted by GitHub Pages; it sets no cookies and runs no analytics, but GitHub records visitors’ IP addresses as any web host does, under GitHub’s privacy statement. The walkthrough video on the home page is served from praxis-hq.ai, so playing it fetches a file from that domain.
